Clinical framework

What clinicians assess

This page organizes the major clinical decision points for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus. It is designed for patient education and professional revision, not as a substitute for a current guideline, local protocol or individualized clinical assessment.

Disease-Specific Clinical Table

Clinical domainReference notes
Diagnosis / assessmentGlucose/HbA1c assessment; renal function; cardiovascular risk
Key monitoringHbA1c and glucose monitoring individualized to treatment
Management principlesLifestyle management plus individualized glucose-lowering therapy
Follow-up / outcomesHbA1c; glucose; renal function; weight; hypoglycaemia risk
Patient educationExplain the diagnosis, purpose of treatment, adherence, lifestyle measures, monitoring requirements and when to seek urgent care. Use teach-back to confirm understanding.
Multidisciplinary rolesDoctors assess and diagnose; pharmacists optimize medicines, identify interactions and support adherence; nurses monitor, educate and coordinate care; patients participate in decisions, take medicines as agreed and report problems promptly.
IndividualizationConsider age, pregnancy, comorbidities, renal/hepatic function, allergies, concurrent medicines, socioeconomic barriers and patient preferences.

Patient Checklist

  • Know the name and purpose of each medicine.
  • Know how and when to take or use treatment.
  • Ask what adverse effects require medical attention.
  • Keep appointments and monitoring tests.
  • Tell the healthcare team about OTC medicines, supplements and allergies.
  • Do not stop long-term treatment abruptly without professional advice unless an emergency clinician instructs otherwise.

🚨 When urgent medical care may be needed

Altered consciousness, severe dehydration, persistent vomiting, suspected DKA/HHS

Emergency symptoms should be assessed urgently rather than managed from this webpage.
